# Energetic properties of objects and spaces

*Reviewed November 2026*

**Research strength — None found** for invisible healing or calming forces in crystals, talismans or sacred room proportions beyond expectation and ordinary sensory design.

**Traditional convergence — Moderate** *(practical guidance on choosing clean, quiet, well-lit rooms appears across contemplative traditions, yet modern crystal healing and commercial energy-object practices are recent)*.

> **In short:** controlled blinded tests show that holding a real crystal feels no different from holding a piece of glass — both produce identical tingling, warmth or calm among people who already expect them to work, and neither helps people who do not.[^1] The same earlier experiment with glass replicas found identical sensations of warmth and tingling, again tracking belief rather than the stone.[^2] The same holds for rooms: in a randomised hospital-room trial of 558 adults, "Golden Ratio" proportions did nothing for anxiety, while room designs only helped insofar as they looked pleasant, included greenery and supported social connection.[^3] Objects and rooms affect human mood through visible, measurable things — lighting, clutter, temperature, greenery and personal association — not invisible forces.[^4]

---

## What it is

Two overlapping beliefs about physical surroundings:

- **Objects with supposed intrinsic forces** — holding, wearing or placing crystals, gemstones, stones, talismans or "charged" items on the body or around a room to absorb tension, block stress or restore emotional balance.[^1]
- **Rooms shaped around unseen spatial rules** — arranging furniture, proportions or compass orientations according to geomantic or sacred-geometry claims rather than ordinary light, ventilation, acoustics and practical layout.[^3]

This page separates those unproven claims from ordinary sensory and environmental factors — air quality, noise, lighting, clutter and nature views — which have clear evidence and are covered on their own pages.[^4]

---

## What it does for you

### Real crystals perform no differently from fake stones under blinded testing

In a 2025 randomised placebo-controlled trial, **138 adults** were first sorted into believers and nonbelievers in crystal healing, then assigned to follow a **14-day protocol** holding either a genuine **rose quartz** crystal or a visually identical placebo stone.[^1] Anxiety fell **only among believers**, with **no statistically significant difference between the real crystal and the placebo** — and nonbelievers showed no reliable improvement.[^1] The authors concluded that anxiety reductions were driven by expectation, conditioning and causal attribution rather than any property of the stone.[^1]

That confirms an earlier blinded experiment with **80 volunteers** meditating for five minutes with either a real quartz crystal or an indistinguishable glass replica: participants could not tell which was real and reported identical sensations of warmth, tingling and relaxation with both, again tracking prior suggestibility and belief.[^2]

### Room design reduces anxiety through ordinary pleasantness, not unseen geometry

In a randomised trial assigning **558 adults** to view a standard single-bed hospital room or one of three redesigns, **Evidence-Based Design** directly reduced anxiety (**d = 0.40**, *p* < 0.001).[^3] By contrast, redesigning the room using **Golden Ratio proportions had no effect on anxiety**, and a **Feng Shui** redesign had no direct effect on anxiety — working only indirectly to the extent that viewers rated the room as more pleasant and supportive.[^3]

Similarly, a systematic review of quantitative Feng Shui studies concluded that empirical evidence remains scarce and uneven, with measurable comfort tied to physical microclimate, daylight, greenery and visual order rather than invisible room currents.[^4]

---

## How much you need to do

- **No dose exists for crystals, talismans or energetic objects**, because blinded trials find zero effect beyond placebo expectation.[^1]
- **For your sitting space**, keep it clean, quiet, comfortably lit and visually uncluttered — which are the observable variables that actually reduce friction and distraction.[^3]

**So: spend nothing on crystals, room-clearing gadgets or paid spatial consultants; if a quiet corner with a single simple object helps remind you to sit down and breathe, use a plain stone, wooden beads or a timer and skip the metaphysics.**[^4]

## Where the evidence stops

**Blinded tests repeatedly reduce crystal effects to expectation.** Whenever participants do not know whether they are holding a genuine stone or a glass imitation, the real crystal shows no advantage on relaxation, physical sensation or anxiety.[^1] An earlier glass-replica meditation study reached the same conclusion.[^2]

**Physical properties of minerals do not translate into human healing.** While quartz crystals exhibit piezoelectricity under mechanical pressure — which makes them useful in watches and electronic oscillators — holding a stone in your palm does not generate therapeutic currents or alter human physiology.[^1]

**Spatial comfort comes from light, air, greenery and layout.** In environmental research, rooms feel calmer when daylight, ventilation, natural plants, sightlines and social comfort are improved — variables already accounted for by architecture and environmental psychology without adding unseen forces.[^3] A Feng Shui review likewise ties measurable comfort to microclimate and visual order rather than invisible currents.[^4]

**Unknowns:** whether holding a smooth stone or bead strand acts as a simple tactile habit cue (like prayer beads) for keeping restless hands still during meditation once belief-based claims are stripped away; and how much real-world room layout affects stress compared with the online photographs tested in hospital-room trials.[^3]

**And one thing to actively disbelieve:** that rose quartz, amethyst, shungite, orgone pyramids or specific room angles absorb stress, shield you from radiation or emit calming waves into your nervous system.

## Who should be careful

Holding an ordinary stone or rearranging furniture carries no direct physical risk. Two practical cautions apply.

*The cautions below are practical judgements rather than trial findings, and are marked as such.*

**Delaying real care or material fixes.** Using crystals, room talismans or "space-clearing" services to cope with persistent anxiety, depression, insomnia or an unsafe living situation delays medical care or practical problem-solving — and can waste substantial money on people already under financial strain.

**Exploitation and toxic or sharp minerals.** Some commercial wellness stones are mined under hazardous labour conditions, and a small number of raw mineral specimens sold online contain heavy metals (such as lead-bearing galena or cinnabar) or sharp fibres. Wash hands after handling unfamiliar raw minerals, keep small stones away from young children, and do not soak unknown minerals in drinking water.

## Traditional convergence

Moderate. Older contemplative manuals *did* care carefully about the space where a person practises — yet they described ordinary sensory comfort rather than invisible energies.

In early India, the *Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad* advised practising in a spot that is level, clean, free of pebbles, sand and noise, sheltered from high wind, and "agreeable to the mind and not offensive to the eye."[^5] Centuries later in Japan, Eihei Dōgen's *Fukan Zazengi* gave similarly practical instructions for a meditation room: quiet, neat and clean, "neither too dark nor too bright, warm in the winter and cool in the summer."[^5] By contrast, modern Western crystal healing and commercial energy-grid practices are largely late-twentieth-century inventions built on top of placebo expectation.[^1] French and colleagues' glass-replica study is part of the same picture.[^2]

You can test what is actually working in ten minutes: tidy one uncluttered corner of a quiet room with comfortable light, sit down holding an ordinary smooth pebble from outside, and notice that any steadiness comes from the quiet room and your own attention — not from what the stone is made of.
